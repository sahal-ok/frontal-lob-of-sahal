ESSAY / 2026

A thought experiment

The next
revolution is
optimization.

What if every major economic revolution is simply humanity making another capability cheaper?

↓

01

Predicting the future is hard.

If anyone could consistently predict the future, they would probably become a trillionaire.

Even the best investors and technology companies make decisions that look completely different in hindsight.

Even SoftBank sold all their stock in Nvidia in October 2025, and then the AI boom came. Even the world's best investors can't predict the future, so predicting the future is extremely hard.

So perhaps the better question isn't:

What will happen?

Instead:

What is humanity continuously trying to optimize?

02

One pattern keeps appearing.

01 Agriculture Food
02 Industry Muscle
03 Internet Knowledge
04 AI Intelligence
05 Agents Execution

Each revolution seems to make a previously expensive capability dramatically cheaper and more accessible.

03 — AGRICULTURE

We optimized food.

Humans learned to produce food systematically. Better crops, seeds, irrigation, fertilizers and farming techniques increased output.

We optimized agriculture.

But optimization exposed another constraint:

Human physical labor.

04 — INDUSTRY

We optimized muscle.

Machines multiplied what one human could physically accomplish.

Steam engines. Factories. Electricity. Cars. Industrial machinery.

Physical production became cheaper and more scalable.

But then we needed knowledge.

05 — INTERNET

We optimized access to knowledge.

The Internet made information accessible to almost anyone with a connection.

Books, research, documentation, lectures and experts became available from almost anywhere.

Knowledge became cheap to access.

But access to knowledge isn't the same as knowing what to do with it.

06 — AI

We are optimizing intelligence.

AI can increasingly reason over information, write code, analyze data, generate ideas and perform cognitive tasks.

The important change may not simply be that AI is becoming smarter.

Intelligence itself is becoming cheaper to access.

And once intelligence becomes cheap, another bottleneck appears.

07 — AGENTS

From thinking to doing.

AI can tell you what to do.

Agents attempt to actually do it.

Research.

Decide.

Execute.

Measure.

Improve.

One agent is useful.

What happens when there are thousands?

The problem becomes coordination.

08 — THE NEXT BOTTLENECK

Optimization of optimization.

Imagine a company with 100,000 agents.

Some research. Some code. Some sell. Some negotiate. Some monitor machines.

You cannot manually coordinate all of them.

You need systems that decide:

Which agent should do what?

When should it act?

Which agents should work together?

When should humans intervene?

How do we measure the outcome?

09 — RESPONSIBILITY

Maybe the next scarce resource is responsibility.

Imagine a world where software, research, marketing, design and execution become extremely cheap.

Simply doing something may no longer be particularly valuable.

Who is responsible for making sure it actually works?

The question changes from:

Can you perform the task?

to:

Can you reliably produce the desired outcome?

10 — OUTCOMES

People don't buy technology.

They buy outcomes.

Someone doesn't necessarily want software. They want the problem solved by the software.

They don't necessarily want an AI agent. They want what the agent accomplishes.

Technology is the mechanism.
The outcome is the value.

11 — HUMANS

What happens to human work?

When automobiles became widespread, the number of horses used for transportation declined.

The transportation system changed.

Robots may eventually change physical work in a similar way.

Some jobs may disappear. Some may shrink. Some may completely change.

The frontier keeps moving.

12 — WEALTH

So why do people pay you?

People have wants.

But there is often a gap between what they want and their ability to obtain it.

That gap creates an opportunity.

Want → Gap → Solution → Value

Someone wants software but doesn't know how to build it. You build it.

Someone wants an agent but doesn't know how to create it. You create it.

Someone wants something listed somewhere but doesn't know how to get it there. You solve the problem.

Sell what people want,
but don't know how to get.

13 — DEMOCRATIZATION

Optimization has a second side.

Democratization.

Agriculture made food production scalable.

Industry made mechanical power accessible.

The Internet made information accessible.

AI is making cognitive capability accessible.

Agents may make autonomous execution accessible.

14 — THE FRAMEWORK

If you want to think about the future, ask five questions.

  1. 01 What is currently expensive?
  2. 02 What happens if it becomes 100× cheaper?
  3. 03 Who gains access?
  4. 04 What new things become possible?
  5. 05 What becomes the next bottleneck?

15 — THE 1% GUESS

Don't try to predict everything.

If I had to make a tiny bet about the future, I would look for technologies that do two things.

01

Optimize an existing capability.

02

Democratize a capability that is currently expensive or difficult to access.

Then look one step further.

Every successful optimization creates a new bottleneck.

What is becoming cheap?

What will become scarce because of it?
